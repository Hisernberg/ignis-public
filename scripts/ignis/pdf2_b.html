The 30-second pitch script

[0–8 s] "MODIS watched Earth burn from orbit for twenty-five years. In 2012, VIIRS joined — sharper eyes, different language, and the record split in two."

[8–20 s] "IGNIS unites them: one harmonized calendar across a quarter century — here's every fire season since 2000 — with a continuity coefficient that makes the two eras scientifically comparable. Live detections map yesterday's burns; a seasonal outlook flags the months ahead."

[20–30 s] "And when activity spikes, the rule engine raises the alert while Hugging Face triage ranks which open events matter for this place. Two NASA missions, one calendar — built on FIRMS, GIBS and EONET, deployed on authentic data end to end."

05Judging Strategy & What Comes After

Why this entry scores, where it can still grow, and how the win compounds.

Scoring against Global judging criteria

Impact (9/10): a decision-relevant memory of fire for ecologists, insurers, journalists and residents — 25 years in one view.

Creativity (8.5/10): harmonization rendered as a calendar is a new visual grammar for data fusion; the outlook and semantic triage extend the brief rather than repeat it.

Validity (9.5/10): every figure traces to a named NASA endpoint; methodology, sampling design and continuity coefficients are published in-app and in-repo; dual-source fallback reproduces the record independently.

Relevance (9/10): built directly on FIRMS/LANCE communities' needs; the runbook and MCP surface make it a reusable capability, not a demo.

Presentation (8.5/10): mission-control identity consistent with the team's 25-page playbook; a rehearsed three-click demo arc.

Post-event roadmap

v1.1 (week 1): exact-count FIRMS mode for the calendar (runbook Phase 2.3); per-month FRP density maps; user-drawn AOIs persisted to SQLite.

v1.2 (month 1): NISAR burn-scar cross-validation (the Dossier 01 closing line becomes a feature); Chronos-Bolt upgrade path for the outlook; subscription-style AOI alerts via webhook.

Community: publish the GIBS-MVT harmonization pipeline as an open notebook so other teams can build calendars for their own regions — judge-visible goodwill that outlives the event.

Why we believe this wins

Winning Space Apps entries share three properties: they answer the challenge's actual intellectual question rather than decorating around it; they work flawlessly in front of judges; and they leave the panel with one unforgettable sentence. IGNIS answers the harmonization question with measured continuity coefficients across eight regions and a fused 25-year record; it works because the entire pipeline was built, broken, fixed and browser-verified before the event began; and its sentence is already written — "two NASA missions, one calendar." The team's constraint set (chat-only AI, API-first, automation-driven) stopped being a limitation and became the demo's spine: zero runtime LLM dependencies, machine-drivable endpoints, and a runbook any reviewer can execute to reproduce the whole system in an hour.

Final instruction to the team: resist adding features on Saturday. The winning move is polish, proof and story. The map is live, the calendar is deep, the outlook is honest, the alert engine never sleeps — show exactly that, nothing less and nothing more.
