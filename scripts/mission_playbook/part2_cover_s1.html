Mission Briefing

Why this document exists, the strategy it encodes, and how to execute it.

Every October, tens of thousands of teams around the world sit down for the same 48 hours, look at the same challenge list, and build from a cold start. Most of them spend the first six hours forming a team, the next six arguing about ideas, and the last twelve panicking about the video submission. The teams that reach Global Judging do the opposite: they arrive with a battle plan, a pre-built codebase, a tested AI stack, and a decision framework that lets them lock a challenge within the first two hours. This playbook is that head start, compressed into a single mission dossier.

The core strategy is One Flagship App. Instead of waiting to react to the challenge list, you will build the chassis of a single, exceptionally strong platform — code-named TERRA SENTINEL — before the event begins. It is engineered around NASA’s most reliable, most spectacular data streams and the strongest open AI models on Hugging Face, and designed with three interchangeable mission skins so that whichever challenge drops, roughly seventy percent of your build is already done. During the hackathon you do not build an app; you adapt, polish, and pitch one.

This document is organized as a flight plan. Sections 01–02 give you intelligence: how judging works and what winners did. Sections 03–04 are the arsenal: every API, dataset, geospatial library, and Hugging Face model worth carrying. Section 05 is the targeting system: a scoring matrix to pick your challenge in under two hours. Section 06 is the flagship blueprint itself. Sections 07–09 are the execution doctrine: a ten-week pre-mission timeline, an hour-by-hour 48-hour battle plan, and the pitch playbook that converts a good build into a nominated project.

OPERATING PRINCIPLE
Judges do not award the most code. They award the clearest story backed by real NASA data, real science, and a live demo that works on the first try. Every decision in this plan optimizes for those three things.

Know the Battlefield

What the NASA International Space Apps Challenge is, how big it is, and the exact path a project travels from submission to the podium.

The NASA International Space Apps Challenge is the world’s largest annual global hackathon, run by NASA with international space agency partners. For two days each October, participants at hundreds of local events — plus a fully online Universal Event — form teams and use NASA’s open data along with partner agency data to build projects against a published set of written challenges. There is no entry fee, no requirement to be a professional developer, and no restriction on open-source tools: what counts is what you demonstrate with the data. In 2025 the event drew participants at 551 local events across 167 countries and territories, and the 2026 edition runs on November 14–15, 2026, with registration already open since August 26, 2026.

1.1 The Judging Funnel — and Why It Matters

Understanding the funnel changes how you allocate effort. After the event closes, judges at each local event nominate their strongest projects as Global Nominees — in 2025 that was roughly 1,290 projects out of more than 11,500 submissions, or about one in nine. Those nominees enter Global Judging, are narrowed to around 40 Global Finalists, and a panel of executive judges at NASA and partner agencies selects the 10 Global Winners. Every layer of that funnel is a human skimming your project page and your 30-second video, usually for less than two minutes. The practical consequence: your project page, video, and data attribution do more work per minute of effort than any additional feature ever will.

1.2 The Ten Global Award Categories

Your project competes for one primary category — you nominate yourself when submitting — but the strongest builds are engineered so that judges could justify them in several. Memorize the ten below; Section 06 maps the flagship app against all of them.

CATEGORY 01
Best Use of Science
Sharpest, most defensible use of the scientific method. Formulas, assumptions, and validation must be stated on the project page.
CATEGORY 02
Best Use of Data
Turning raw data into insight. The winners here process real, often messy NASA datasets and visibly transform them, not just embed them.
CATEGORY 03
Best Use of Technology
The boldest, most elegant technical execution — architecture, AI stack, and engineering quality under hackathon constraints.
CATEGORY 04
Galactic Impact
Projects that could change life on Earth or in space at planetary scale: climate resilience, disaster response, democratized access.
CATEGORY 05
Best Mission Concept
The clearest plan to evolve a hackathon prototype into a real mission, product, or research program with named stakeholders.
CATEGORY 06
Most Inspiring Storytelling
The build that makes people feel the wonder of space and Earth science — narrative, visuals, and emotional arc.
CATEGORY 07
Best Use of Hardware
Creative integration of physical hardware with space data. Optional lane for teams with electronics skills.
CATEGORY 08
Global Connection
Projects that make the data usable across borders, languages, and communities — localization, accessibility, collaboration.
CATEGORY 09
Art & Technology
Fusion of creative practice with space science: data art, generative visuals, sound, and interactive experiences.
CATEGORY 10
Community Choice
Voted by the global participant community. Earned by shareable, delightful, easy-to-understand demos.
STRATEGIC NOTE
The flagship app in Section 06 is designed to be self-nominated for Best Use of Data or Best Use of Technology while remaining defensible in six other categories. Depth in one lane, coverage everywhere else.
