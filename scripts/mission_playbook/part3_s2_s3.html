SECTION 02 // INTELLIGENCE
Winner DNA Teardown

What recent Global Winners actually built, and the six patterns you can replicate before the countdown starts.

Across 2024 and 2025, the ten Global Winners each year were selected from fields of roughly ten thousand projects. The winning builds of the last two cycles — teams like WMPGang (Best Use of Science, 2024) and 2025’s cohort tackling commercializing low Earth orbit, meteor analysis, and space weather storytelling — look very different on the surface. But beneath the domain differences, the same engineering and presentation decisions repeat almost every year. This section extracts those decisions so you can bake them in from commit one, instead of trying to retrofit polish in hour forty-four.

2.1 Six Repeatable Winning Patterns
PATTERN 01 // DATA DEPTH
Real pipelines, not screenshots
Winners process at least one raw or near-real-time dataset end to end — fetching, cleaning, transforming, and analyzing it live. A map tile layer is table stakes; a computed insight derived from two fused datasets is what gets nominated.
PATTERN 02 // AI WOVEN IN
AI as an engine, not garnish
Recent winners increasingly run a concrete model or LLM step inside the product — classification, forecasting, or natural-language explanation — and can name the model, its input/output, and why it fits. Vague “we used AI” claims score nothing.
PATTERN 03 // LIVE DEMO
It must work on the first click
Judges spend about two minutes. Winners host a deployed URL that loads fast, shows something visually striking in under five seconds, and never requires signup. Local fallback video protects against venue wifi failure.
PATTERN 04 // NARROW STORY
One user, one problem, one outcome
The strongest projects serve a specific persona — a coastal planner, a farmer, an astronomer — and state the outcome in one sentence. Broad “platform for everyone” framing consistently loses to a sharp single story told well.
PATTERN 05 // SCIENCE VISIBLE
Method on the page
Best Use of Science winners document assumptions, formulas, and validation right on the project page. Cite the exact dataset names and versions. Make a judge able to verify one claim in sixty seconds.
PATTERN 06 // DELIGHT
A moment people screenshot
Community Choice and storytelling awards go to builds with one unforgettable moment: a cinematic globe zoom, a narrated replay, a generated image of tomorrow’s sky. Plan and protect this moment; it is a feature, not decoration.

TEARDOWN DIRECTIVE
Before the event, spend one hour reading the current Global Winners pages on spaceappschallenge.org and watch their videos. Judge each one against the six patterns above. You are calibrating your own taste to the actual standard — the single highest-leverage hour of prep.
SECTION 03 // ARSENAL
The Arsenal: APIs & Data Catalog

Every NASA data source worth carrying into battle, the geospatial library stack that processes it, and the thirty-minute key setup drill.

NASA operates one of the largest open data estates on Earth — petabytes spanning Earth observation, heliophysics, planetary science, and astrophysics. You cannot use all of it, and you should not try. The catalog below is filtered down to the sources that are live-queryable, hackathon-friendly, and visually spectacular: they return JSON or map tiles in seconds, require no credentials beyond a free key, and have powered winning projects in past cycles. Your flagship app will be built on the first table and pull from the rest opportunistically.

3.4 The 30-Minute Key Setup Drill

Do this during Week 1 of the pre-mission timeline, not during the hackathon. Each step is free and takes minutes, but Earthdata approvals can take hours — which is exactly the kind of problem you never want to meet at hour 20 of the event.

  1. api.nasa.gov key — sign up at api.nasa.gov, receive the key instantly by email; it unlocks EONET, EPIC, DONKI, NeoWs, and Exoplanet Archive endpoints. Respect the 30 req/s and 50,000 req/day limits.
  2. Earthdata Login — create the account, complete profile, and accept the common EULAs so FIRMS and CMR-linked downloads work without friction.
  3. FIRMS map key — request a MAP_KEY from the FIRMS site for tile-based fire overlays; activate it and test a VIIRS layer in MapLibre.
  4. Hugging Face account — create the account, generate a read token, and confirm you can load one model from Section 04 locally or on a Space.
  5. Smoke-test everything — a single script that calls all five credential-dependent sources and prints OK. Keep it in the repo; run it again the night before the event.
