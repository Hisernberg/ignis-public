SECTION 09 // EXECUTION
Pitch & Submission Playbook

The last mile: a 30-second video structure, the project page judges read, and the checklist that catches everything.

By the time a judge opens your submission, the outcome is mostly decided by two artifacts you control completely: the video and the project page. They are the only part of your work almost every judge in the funnel will actually experience, from local nomination to global finals. Treat them as features with the same rigor as code — scripted, rehearsed, and quality-checked — and you convert the same 48 hours of engineering into roughly twice the perceived quality.

9.1 The 30-Second Video Doctrine
SECONDSBEATEXECUTION NOTES
0–5The HookCold open on Story Mode — the EPIC-to-street zoom — with one spoken line: “Right now, N things are happening on Earth. We built mission control for the planet.” No logos, no title cards, no “hi, we are team X.”
5–20The Live DemoThree cuts maximum: the ops map with event pulses, the AI briefing appearing in real time, the forecast curve drawing. Each cut under 5 seconds, each showing one feature doing something no static image could fake.
20–27The EvidenceOne screen of the Data Ledger and the validation metric — proof this runs on real NASA data and honest numbers. Voiceover states the data sources by name.
27–30The ImpactFinal line over the spinning globe: the persona outcome from your mission statement. End on the URL, held for a full second. Team name appears once, quietly, bottom corner.

9.2 Project Page Anatomy — What Judges Read in Two Minutes
Mission statement (top, 1 sentence) — the persona, the problem, the outcome. If a judge reads nothing else, they must get this.
Live demo link first — above the fold, incognito-friendly, no signup. The video sits immediately next to it for the wifi-worst case.
How it works (3 bullets) — data in, AI processing, decision out — with the exact NASA datasets named, not “NASA data.”
Science & validation paragraph — method, one formula, the per-model metric, and assumptions. This is what separates Best Use of Science nominees.
Mission concept paragraph — who adopts this next week, next year: named user types (emergency-management office, newsroom, school program) and the zero-cost pilot path.
Data credits & licenses — full ledger with timestamps. Honest attribution reads as integrity; judges notice who did it.
Team & roles — first names, one line each on what they built. Judges fund teams that look like teams, not lone geniuses.
What’s next (2 lines) — the single most credible next milestone, not a startup fantasy. Ambition with a roadmap beats ambition alone.
9.3 Final Submission QC — Run at H+45, Together, Aloud
Incognito test — demo URL opens in a fresh incognito window, first meaningful paint under 3 seconds, zero console errors on the demo path.
Device test — the same path on a phone and on the actual laptop that will present at the local event.
Field completeness — every portal field filled, challenge selected, category nominated, all teammates on the roster, video plays from the portal embed.
Attribution audit — every third-party asset and dataset credited; NASA content guidelines and marking rules followed for imagery.
Story coherence — video, page, and live demo all state the same mission sentence. One story, three surfaces, zero drift.
Submission receipt — screenshot the confirmation. Then close the laptop: no further commits touch the demo path before judging.
THE PRESENTATION PRIME DIRECTIVE
When you pitch live, demo first, slides never. Open on the globe, narrate the mission sentence, perform one briefing live, and land the impact line. If anything fails, switch to the backup video within three seconds and keep narrating — composure under failure is itself what judges remember.
9.4 The Two-Minute Local Pitch Script

Most local events end with a short live pitch to the room’s judges, who then nominate Global Nominees from what they saw on stage. Two minutes is the standard slot. The script below is engineered around the funnel logic from Section 01: local judges nominate projects that are demonstrated, not described. Rehearse it in Week 9 exactly as written, then let each teammate deliver it once so anyone can step in.

CLOCKBEATWHAT HAPPENS ON STAGE
0:00–0:20Cold openScreen already on the live globe. One line: the mission sentence. No introductions, no agenda slide — the product is the intro.
0:20–1:00Live demoClick one event; the briefing generates; the forecast curve draws. Narrate what the judge is seeing, never how it was built.
1:00–1:30Data & scienceName the three NASA streams and one validation metric. Show the Data Ledger for five seconds — credibility is visual.

1:50–2:00CloseFinal line: “Mission control for planet Earth — built this weekend on NASA’s open data.” Hold the globe on screen. Stop talking.
