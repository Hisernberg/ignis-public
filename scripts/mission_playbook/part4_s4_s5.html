SECTION 04 // ARSENAL
AI Division: Hugging Face Registry

Eight curated models that turn NASA data into insight — with exact IDs, roles, and the deployment route for each.

AI separates nominees from winners in the current cycle, but only when it is load-bearing: a named model with a defined input, a defined output, and a visible effect on the user experience. The registry below is engineered around that standard. It deliberately includes Prithvi-EO-2.0 — NASA’s own open geospatial foundation model, built with IBM — because telling judges that your project runs on NASA’s AI on top of NASA’s data is a narrative advantage no generic GPT wrapper can match. Every model listed is open-weight, runs free on CPU or a small GPU, and can be embedded in a hackathon build within hours.

4.2 Deployment Doctrine — Three Routes
ROUTE A // RECOMMENDED
HF Spaces + Gradio microservices
Each model gets a Space that exposes one clean endpoint (JSON in, JSON out). Your Next.js backend calls them. Free CPU tier is enough for Chronos, MiniLM, and Whisper; zero infra to babysit during judging.
ROUTE B // FALLBACK
Local inference server
Run transformers or ONNX Runtime on one laptop serving the team’s endpoints on the LAN. Immune to venue wifi; required only for Prithvi if you fine-tune beyond the free tier.
ROUTE C // STRETCH
transformers.js in-browser
MiniLM and small classifiers run directly in the browser via ONNX WASM. Nothing to host, instant demo, but keep heavy models server-side.
VALIDATION RULE
Every AI claim gets one number
For each model step, compute one honest validation metric on real data (e.g., forecast MAE vs. persistence baseline, segmentation IoU on a labeled tile). One number per model — this is what Best Use of Science judges probe.
AI INTEGRATION BUDGET
Integrate at most three of these models during the 48 hours (recommended: Chronos-Bolt, MiniLM retrieval, LLM briefer — with Prithvi pre-wired in prep). Every additional model is a +2h cost and a new failure surface. The registry exists so you choose deliberately, not greedily.
SECTION 05 // TARGETING
Target Selection Framework

A weighted scoring system that locks your challenge choice within two hours of release — with zero team arguments.

When the challenge list drops, teams that debate ideas for six hours are already losing. The list is typically 15–20 written challenges spanning Earth science, climate, heliophysics, astrophysics, and human spaceflight, and each one is more flexible than its title suggests. Your job is not to find “the perfect challenge” — it is to score quickly, decide, and start adapting. The matrix below turns that decision into arithmetic that two to four people can execute calmly, because the criteria and weights were agreed weeks in advance.

5.1 The Scoring Matrix

Score each candidate challenge 1–5 on five criteria, multiply by the weight, and sum. The weights encode what the funnel analysis in Section 01 showed: data and demo quality dominate, raw topic familiarity matters least because your stack adapts.

CRITERIONWEIGHTSCORE 5 MEANS…
Data availability0.30The challenge maps directly onto 2+ arsenal sources from Section 03 with live, queryable, spectacular data — ideally EONET/GIBS/FIRMS class.
Demo wow0.25You can picture a 30-second video that makes a stranger lean in: a globe zoom, a forecast curve, a segmentation animation.
AI leverage0.20At least two registry models from Section 04 plug in naturally (forecasting, segmentation, retrieval, briefing).
Story strength0.15One named persona with a real stakes narrative (a farmer before harvest, a city during a heat wave, a family during a storm).
Competition density0.10Challenge reads niche or technical — fewer than ~5% of teams will pick it, and judge fatigue will not work against you.
FORMULA: SCORE = Σ(CRITERION × WEIGHT). DECIDE BY H+2; HIGHEST SCORE WINS UNLESS IT IS WITHIN 0.2 OF RUNNER-UP, THEN CAPTAIN TIE-BREAKS IMMEDIATELY.
