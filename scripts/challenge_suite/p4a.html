Mission Briefing

The third armed option: the portfolio’s humanitarian lane, and its safest data story.

ROOTLOGIC addresses Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data — create a decision-support tool that uses NASA Earth observations along with local soil information, crop characteristics, and farmer priorities to help farmers explore crop-rotation strategies that could strengthen soil health and adapt their farms to changing conditions. In the portfolio’s division of labor, PYROCHRON attacks the data trophy and PULSE the science trophy; ROOTLOGIC attacks the human ones — Global Connection and Most Inspirational — while doubling as the portfolio’s safest fallback because every input it needs is served by quota-free, lightweight APIs. Where the other two plans visualize planetary truth, this one changes a decision on a real farm. That difference is the pitch.

This document follows the same doctrine as its siblings: the decoded brief (01), the agronomy decision model (02), the data arsenal (03), the product definition (04), the AI division and architecture (05), award lanes and prep timeline (06), the 48-hour battle plan and pitch kit (07–08), and the risk register (09). It reuses the same pre-built chassis — shell, map stack, pipeline pattern, AI services — and adds exactly one novel organ: the rotation rule engine, specified fully in Section 02. The plan assumes the standard pre-build split and keeps ROOTLOGIC self-sufficient enough to be executed by a two-person squad if the team ever splits.

SECTION 01 // THE BRIEF, DECODED
What the Challenge Actually Demands

Four inputs, one output, and a user who must be trusted.

The brief enumerates its own bill of materials. NASA Earth observations — temperature, precipitation, drought indices, vegetation condition. Local soil information — texture, organic matter, drainage. Crop characteristics — rooting depth, water demand, nitrogen behavior, disease families. And farmer priorities — the clause most teams will underweight: the tool must let the user weigh water conservation against soil health against resilience, and visibly honor that weighting in its advice. The output is not a prescription; the verbs are explore and could — a decision-support tool presents defensible options with reasoning, and the farmer decides. Tools that lecture lose; tools that explain and yield win.

The judging read: this brief hides in the Arts & Multimedia subject tag, but it is an applied-science challenge with a storytelling surface. The dual Advanced/Intermediate flag predicts a modest field of earnest entries and very few polished ones — polish is precisely what a pre-built chassis buys. The strongest credibility move available: ground the demo in one fully worked region with real soil polygons, real NASA weather history, and a realistic crop catalog, then show the same engine working for a second, climatically opposite region to prove generality. One deep, one broad — that is the validity story.
