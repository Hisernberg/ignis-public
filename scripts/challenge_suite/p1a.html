SECTION 01 // THE 2026 SLATE
The Battlefield: Fourteen Briefs, One Map

What NASA’s 2026 challenge list reveals before a single line of code is written.

The 2026 slate divides into three recognizable families. The largest is Earth intelligence: six challenges (#02, #06, #07, #09, #10, #14) ask teams to turn NASA’s Earth-observing fleet — MODIS, VIIRS, SMAP, NISAR, POWER, and the Earth Information Center — into insight someone can act on. The second family is exploration operations: seven challenges (#01, #03, #04, #05, #08, #11, #13) orbit the return to the Moon, life on Mars, astronaut health, and the engineering trade-offs of real missions. The third, smaller family is frontier astrophysics: #12’s Planet X hunt with SPHEREx, plus storytelling missions like #01 that treat the whole solar system as a narrative canvas. NASA is explicitly showcasing its newest assets this year — NISAR (launched 2025, now publishing), SPHEREx (all-sky infrared surveys), and the CLPS commercial landers headed for the lunar south pole — which is a relevance signal worth reading.

The difficulty mix matters as much as the topics. Only two challenges are flagged Advanced only (#02, #12). Six are dual-flagged Advanced / Intermediate (#04, #06, #07, #08, #09, #10) — NASA expects real rigor there but has left the door open. Four are Intermediate with a Beginner/Youth pathway (#01, #03, #13, #14), which predicts large, crowded fields of earnest but technically shallow entries: the competition-density penalty for those briefs is real. Subject tags confirm the tilt: Earth Science appears on six briefs, Software on eight, Space Exploration on five, Planets & Moons on six, and Games or Arts & Multimedia on four. For a full-stack team that pre-built a NASA-data mission-control platform, the arithmetic is blunt: the center of gravity of this slate sits exactly on our chassis.
