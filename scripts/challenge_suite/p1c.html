Six briefs point at NASA’s Earth fleet. Scored in ranked order; every data claim verified against live NASA archives.

#09 Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots ADV+INTEARTH ◆ SOFTWARE
The brief states the problem with unusual precision: satellites have tracked active fire for two decades, but the record is split across sensors that cannot be compared directly, and fire managers lack one consistent picture of when and where burning occurs. The deliverable is explicit — a web application that harmonizes the records into a burning activity calendar for a chosen area of interest, usable for early warning and response. This is the best-in class setup on the whole slate: a bounded science problem (sensor bias correction), a concrete product (a calendar), and a user (responders, scientists, land managers). The data reality is flawless: MODIS thermal anomalies archived since November 2000, VIIRS 375 m since 20 January 2012, both free via FIRMS with historical archive access. Our pre-built FIRMS pipeline, map stack, and Chronos forecasting module land on this brief with almost zero translation. It is the flagship — master plan in SP-2026-003.

#02 Be An Earth System Trend Detective! ADVANCEDEARTH ◆ SOFTWARE
This brief asks teams to find variables measured by NASA missions or produced by NASA models, visualize how they change over time, and determine what is changing, where, how much, and whether the change is statistically significant — including the delightfully sharp detail that the same process can trend opposite ways in different regions. It is a rigor showcase: Mann-Kendall trend tests, Sen’s slope, autocorrelation awareness, and honest p-values are the actual product. The variable catalog is enormous and free — SST and chlorophyll from MODIS/VIIRS ocean color, NO₂ columns from OMI/TEMPO, soil moisture from SMAP, water storage anomalies from GRACE-FO, precipitation from GPM — all queryable through Earthdata and open APIs. The visual language (trend maps, significance mosaics, regional contrast stories) suits a map-first team. Its twin-pivot role: roughly seventy percent of the engine built for #09 — ingestion, time-series storage, trend statistics, map shell — snaps directly onto this brief. Master plan in SP-2026-004.

#06 Dancing with the SARs (NISAR) ADV+INTEARTH SCIENCE
Build an interactive application using NISAR radar data to track and visualize surface change — wetland loss, wildfires, earthquakes, glaciers, agriculture. The wow ceiling is the highest on the slate: interferometric deformation maps and change stacks are jaw-dropping when they work. And the data situation has transformed since the last event: NISAR L-band products began public release in January–February 2026, with 100K+ Level 1–3 products already flowing through the Alaska Satellite Facility DAAC and the full science record expected by end of 2026. The catch is processing weight: single-look complex products are terabyte-scale, InSAR pairs require careful baseline selection, and on-demand processing (ASF HyP3) queues can be slow on a hackathon weekend. A team without prior SAR experience burns Saturday fighting GAMMA or ISCE. Scores as high wow, high risk — reserve bench with a defined swap trigger: attempt only if SAR familiarity is confirmed before the event.

#07 Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data ADV+INTARTS ◆ EARTH ◆ SOFTWARE
Create a decision-support tool that uses NASA Earth observations plus local soil information, crop characteristics, and farmer priorities to explore crop-rotation strategies that protect soil health and adapt to shifting climate conditions. This is the humanitarian heavyweight of the slate: food security, water scarcity, and climate adaptation for the people who feed everyone. The data stack is strong and quota-friendly — NASA POWER daily agroclimatology, SMAP soil moisture (with USDA’s Crop-CASMA as precedent), GPM precipitation, MODIS/VIIRS vegetation indices, SoilGrids and USDA soil properties, and the Cropland Data Layer for rotation history. The science is rule-based and explainable: nitrogen fixation, disease-break cycles, water demand, soil cover. Judges reward decisions that visibly honor user priorities — the brief literally demands a priorities input. The demo is less cinematic than fire maps, but the pitch writes itself. Third pick, impact lane — master plan in SP-2026-005.

#10 Identify Earth Locations that Analog Moon Base & Mars Sites ADV+INT+YTHEARTH ◆ MOONS ◆ SOFTWARE
Use open Earth, Moon, and Mars data to identify and characterize new terrestrial analog sites — deserts, polar regions, volcanic terrains, caves — that share conditions with future lunar or Martian base locations. Genuinely cross-planetary: SRTM and ASTER topography against MOLA Mars elevation and LRO lunar models, climate normals against THEMIS and Diviner surface temperatures, geology against spectral maps. The science is multi-criteria similarity scoring, which our stack can express beautifully as weighted overlays and similarity radars per candidate site. It earns an A− rather than a pick for one reason: the analysis surface area is huge. Doing it credibly in 48 hours requires pre-computing the entire similarity pipeline pre-event — which is possible, but competes for the same pre-build hours as #09, #02 and #07. High originality ceiling for a team with GIS depth; park on the bench with a pre-event feasibility probe.

#14 The Earth Information Jukebox INT+YOUTHARTS ◆ EARTH ◆ SOFTWARE
Pair Earth Information Center visual frames with real-time sonification — translating sight into sound so Earth science reaches audiences visualization alone misses. The Art & Technology award lane is practically painted on the brief, and the demo has viral energy: a planet whose temperature anomaly, ice loss, or storm tracks become generative music. The engineering is tractable — Web Audio API synthesis, EIC/SVS frames and public indicator datasets — but the craft is audio design, and quality is brutally exposed: a mediocre sonification sounds like a dial-up modem, while a good one needs musical judgment our team does not disproportionately own. The youth-friendly flag predicts a crowded, playful field. Delightful brief; wrong toolkit. Skipped without regret — but if the event-day team includes a musician, re-score on the spot.
