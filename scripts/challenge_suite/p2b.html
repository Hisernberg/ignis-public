SECTION 02 // THE SCIENCE
Why the Records Clash — and How to Fuse Them

The harmonization method is the intellectual core of the submission. Here it is, end to end.

MODIS (Terra since November 2000, Aqua since 2002) and VIIRS (Suomi NPP since 20 January 2012, NOAA-20 since 2018) both detect thermal anomalies — hot pixels — but they were never designed to be one dataset. MODIS burns 1 km pixels through a contextual algorithm tuned over two decades; VIIRS reads 375 m I-bands that resolve fires up to four times smaller. Their overpass times differ by hours (burns that peak mid-afternoon are caught differently by a 10:30 platform than a 13:30 one), their detection sensitivities differ by land cover, and their Fire Radiative Power retrievals are not interchangeable. Naively stacking “MODIS 2000–2011, VIIRS 2012–now” produces a calendar whose step-change in 2012 is an artifact of the sensors, not of fire on the ground — precisely the illusion the challenge asks us to remove.

02.1 The PYROCHRON Harmonization Method
STEP 1Unify detections into fire events
Cluster raw hotspot pixels into fire events — spatio-temporal clusters (same fire within a radius and a time window) using H₃ hexagonal binning and temporal linking. Working on events rather than pixels removes per-pixel noise and gives the calendar a stable unit: event-days per cell.
OUTPUT: EVENT TABLE — EVENT_ID, SENSOR, TIME, H3 CELL, FRP, CONFIDENCE
STEP 2Exploit the overlap period
Both sensors observe 2012–present. In every H₃ cell and land-cover class, compute the sensor transfer function: how VIIRS event-day counts map to MODIS-equivalent counts (a quantile-mapping / scaling fit per stratum). This overlap is our calibration laboratory — no invented coefficients, everything derived from the data itself.
OUTPUT: STRATIFIED TRANSFER FUNCTIONS (CELL ◆ LAND COVER ◆ MONTH)
STEP 3Back-extend VIIRS, forward-project MODIS
Apply the transfer function backward: pre-2012 MODIS-only years are expressed on the VIIRS-equivalent scale (and vice versa where useful). Uncertainty bands ride along from the transfer-function residuals, so every historical week in the calendar carries an honest confidence ribbon.
OUTPUT: ONE CONTINUOUS 2000–2026 WEEKLY SERIES PER CELL, WITH CI
STEP 4Validate against burned area truth
Active fire counts are detections; MCD64A1 monthly burned area is ground truth for extent. Correlate our harmonized calendar against MCD64A1 per region and per year; publish the correlation map in-app. This validation page is what separates a science submission from a visualization toy — judges can verify us in one click.
OUTPUT: VALIDATION PANEL — r, BIAS, AND KNOWN LIMITATIONS, IN-APP
WHAT WE DO NOT CLAIM
The calendar measures fire activity (detection density), not hectares burned. The methodology page says this in plain language, including limitations (small burns under the MODIS floor pre-2012, persistent clouds, latency at the near-real-time edge). Judges reward honesty more than heroics.

03.1 Engineering Reality Check
  • Volume strategy. Global MCD14ML + VNP14IMGML CSVs for 25 years are manageable when reduced on ingest: pre-aggregate to H₃ cell × day counts and FRP sums in Parquet. Target a static events.parquet of a few GB — served from disk, not fetched live. The live FIRMS API is used only for the “last 7 days” freshness layer.
  • Processing stack. Python: pandas + pyarrow for columnar crunch, geopandas + h3 for spatial semantics, scipy.stats for the quantile-mapping fit. Everything runs on a laptop; nothing needs a cluster if aggregation is done at ingest.
  • Pre-event prefetch. The full archive is downloaded and processed in Weeks 3–5 of the prep timeline. During the event, zero downloads are required — the single biggest risk elimination this plan makes.
  • Freshness path. FIRMS near-real-time feeds (24/48h/7d CSV) cover the event weekend itself, so the live demo can include “fires detected this week” without touching the archive.
