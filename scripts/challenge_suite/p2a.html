What this document contains and how the team consumes it before and during the event.

PYROCHRON is the primary submission: a web application that turns NASA’s fragmented satellite fire record into one harmonized, explorable, forecast-ready burning activity calendar. This master plan is the complete engineering and strategy doctrine for building it — written before the event, so the 48 hours are spent executing rather than deciding. SECTION 01 // THE BRIEF, DECODED
What the Challenge Actually Demands

Reading the brief the way a judge will grade it.

The official text asks for a web application that harmonizes the MODIS and VIIRS active-fire records into a burning activity calendar based on active fire hotspots, enabling early warning and emergency responders, scientists, and land managers to examine historical fire patterns, unusual conditions, and critical periods within a selected area of interest. Four verbs carry the grade: harmonize (the science), calendar (the product), examine (the interactivity), and enable (the user relevance). Teams that only visualize raw FIRMS points will fail the first verb; teams that harmonize but produce a static report will fail the second and third. The winning shape is a live app with an honest, documented fusion method underneath.

The brief also names its users explicitly — early warning systems, emergency responders, scientists, land managers — which hands us the persona slide for the pitch: each screen we ship should be traceable to one of those four users. The phrase “critical periods” is the strongest hint: the calendar must not only show history but surface when fire activity is abnormal relative to climatology, which is exactly what our z-score anomaly engine and Chronos-Bolt forecast provide. Finally, the dual difficulty flag (Advanced / Intermediate) tells us the judge pool expects methodological transparency but not a peer-reviewed algorithm — a defensible, well-documented bias-correction approach with validation statistics clears the bar with room to spare.
