SECTION 02 // THE DECISION MODEL
Twelve Explainable Rules

The rotation engine is deliberately rule-based: transparent, auditable, and computable in milliseconds.

ROOTLOGIC’s engine scores a candidate rotation sequence (a multi-year ordered crop plan) against a field profile using twelve rules grouped into four families. Rules return a subscore plus a plain-language trace line — the sentence the interface shows the farmer. The total score is the weighted sum where the weights come from the farmer’s priority sliders, so the same data produces different rankings for Amara and Daniel, visibly and honestly. This is the anti-black-box stance: no learned model stands between the farmer and the reasoning; the AI layer narrates the rules’ output rather than replacing it.

The engineering posture matches the other plans: everything the demo needs is pre-fetched and cached before the event — one worked region’s five-year daily weather is a few megabytes; soil rasters for a region are trivial. The live POWER call runs during the demo only to prove freshness, with the cached copy as an immediate fallback. This challenge has the lightest data weight of the three, which is why ROOTLOGIC is the portfolio’s designated safe harbor.

SECTION 04 // THE PRODUCT
ROOTLOGIC — Screens and the Signature Move

Four screens; the second one is where priorities become visible physics.

Screen 1 — Field Profile. The farmer (or judge) picks a region — preset fields in the Sahel, US Midwest, and Mediterranean — and sees the field’s live dossier: five-year rainfall trace from GPM/POWER, soil texture card from SoilGrids, recent crop history from CDL where available, and the current seasonal outlook. Everything is sourced on-screen. Screen 2 — Rotation Explorer. The priority sliders (water, soil health, resilience, cost) sit at the top; below, ranked rotation options render as cards — each card a mini timeline of the sequence with its total score, and — the signature move — a trace list of which rules fired: passed in green, failed in red, partial in amber, each phrased in plain words. Dragging a slider re-scores live; the reordering is the demo moment where the brief’s “farmer priorities” clause becomes visible.

Screen 3 — Season Outlook. The chosen rotation meets the year ahead: POWER-based seasonal anomaly context, SMAP start-of-season moisture, and a Chronos-Bolt projection of the rainfall series with explicit “projection” labeling — advice framed as conditions to watch, not predictions to trust. Screen 4 — Field Notebook. A shareable one-page summary: the field, the chosen rotation, the rule traces, the data sources with links — printable, and honest about what the tool does not know (pest pressure, market prices, seed availability) with pointers to where the farmer gets that knowledge. Saying what we don’t know is a trust feature, and trust is this challenge’s real currency.

04.1 Demo Choreography (70 Seconds)

Open on Amara’s Sahel field: the dossier builds itself — a 20-year rainfall trace from POWER, the soil card from SoilGrids, the crop history row. The Rotation Explorer ranks four sequences; the top card’s trace list glows green on three rules. Drag “water conservation” up: cards reorder in place, and the trace on the promoted option explains the earlier-maturing variety choice. Switch region to Daniel’s Midwest farm: the same engine, a different answer, citing cover-crop windows from the NDVI record — generality proven in one click. Finish on the Field Notebook: one printable page, every number linked to its source, and one honest line about what the tool does not know. Close-up on that line for two seconds; it is the most persuasive sentence in the demo.
