SECTION 04 // THE PRODUCT
PYROCHRON — The Global Burning Calendar

One screen tells the whole story: twenty-five fire years in a single grid, for anywhere on Earth.

PYROCHRON’s hero artifact is the Fire Calendar: the user draws or selects an area of interest, and the app renders a 25 × 52 heatmap — years as rows (2000–2026), weeks-of-year as columns — where color encodes harmonized burn-activity intensity. The eye does the science instantly: fire seasons march diagonally, El Niño years light up, a suppressed pandemic year goes dark, and the current year’s row extends week by week. A sensor toggle lets the viewer switch between raw MODIS, raw VIIRS, and the harmonized series — making the 2012 seam appear and vanish on demand. That toggle is the 30-second demo moment: it shows the problem and the fix in one gesture.

04.2 Demo Choreography (90 Seconds)

Open on California. The calendar fills row by row with an animated sweep — two decades of fire history in four seconds. Toggle to raw sensors: the 2012 seam splits the grid; toggle back: one continuous record. Jump to Anomaly Spotlight: the world map shows this week’s five most unusual fire regions. Select one, open Season Outlook: the forecast curve with its band. Finish on the briefing, read one sentence aloud, close on the methodology page. Each beat maps to a judging criterion — nothing in the demo exists for decoration.

Four Models, One Rule: Compute First, Narrate Second

Every AI output is generated from statistics the app has already computed — never invented.

The AI division earns the Best Use of Technology conversation without gambling the submission on it. Chronos-Bolt is the load-bearing model: forecasting is a genuine early-warning capability and needs no training run — the zero-shot recipe was validated in the playbook’s model registry. The LLM briefing is deliberately narrow: it narrates computed truth. This design survives the judge question “is the AI doing real work?” with a yes that we can demonstrate live, and survives the harder question “can it hallucinate?” with a no that we can show in code.

The decisive architectural choice is on Layer 2: everything heavy is pre-computed before the event and shipped as Parquet. The live app reads aggregates, never raw archives, which means the demo cannot be broken by a NASA bandwidth hiccup — the only live calls are GIBS tiles, the 7-day FIRMS freshness feed, and our own microservices. Layer 3 models are cached locally in the container; the Hugging Face dependency ends at build time. This is how a data-intensive submission becomes demo-bulletproof.
